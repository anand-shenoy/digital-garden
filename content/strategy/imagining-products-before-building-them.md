# Imagining Products Before Building Them

**Type:** Concept / method

**Added:** 8 November 2026

## The Idea
Before anyone writes code or spends money, tell the story of the product as if it already exists. Picture a day in someone's life with it, or write the press release for its launch. If the story doesn't work, the product probably won't either, and a story is far cheaper to change than software.

Four sources shape how I use this.

## Knowledge Navigator (Apple, 1987)
In 1987 Apple made a short concept film called the Knowledge Navigator. A university professor comes home, and a tablet the size of a large book greets him with a talking assistant. The assistant reads his messages, finds research and sets up a video call with a colleague. The film imagined a computer of 2009, twenty years ahead.

It came from John Sculley's Apple, after a challenge from Alan Kay. Steve Jobs had left the company two years earlier. Many of its ideas later turned up as the iPad, FaceTime and Siri. The film was never a product plan. Its job was to let people feel the future before they argued about it.

Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Navigator), and [Hugh Dubberly](https://www.dubberly.com/?p=24), who worked on it.

## Two quotes
> "The best way to predict the future is to invent it." Alan Kay

> "You've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." Steve Jobs, WWDC 1997

Jobs said this in a Q&A just after he returned to Apple, when a developer told him he did not understand technology. ([Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2014/02/working_backwards))

## Design fiction
The writer Bruce Sterling coined the term in 2005. The designer Julian Bleecker established it in a 2009 essay, written during a snowy weekend in Helsinki. Sterling's definition: "the deliberate use of diegetic prototypes to suspend disbelief about change". In plainer words, it means objects and stories from a world that doesn't exist yet, made real enough that people react to them as if it did.

Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_fiction), [Bleecker's essay](https://nearfuturelaboratory.com/essays/2009/design-fiction-a-short-essay-on-design-science-fact-and-fiction/).

## Working Backwards (Amazon)
Amazon's version of the same idea is a workshop format. Before asking for a budget or a team, the product owner writes two documents:
- a one page press release, dated in the future, announcing the finished product;
- a list of questions and answers, covering what customers and the business would ask.

If the press release isn't compelling, the product isn't either. Rewriting a page is quicker than rebuilding a product. The method is set out in Colin Bryar and Bill Carr's book *Working Backwards* (2021).

Source: [Koji guide to the PR/FAQ](https://www.koji.so/docs/working-backwards-pr-faq-guide).

## How I use it
I call mine dream walks. I dictate an idea while walking, then read it back in the evening as a short story set a year or so from now. Each story names the product it imagines.

## Why It's Worth Keeping
Architects already do this for clients. A render is a design fiction for a building that hasn't been built. Using the same method on our own tools and processes is a cheap way to find out whether an idea deserves to be built, before any building starts.
